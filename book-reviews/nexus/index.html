"Nexus: A Brief History of Information Networks from the Stone Age to AI"

by Yuval Noah Harari

  • Read on 2026-11-10
  • Rating: ▲▲▲△△
  • Format: Audio (17 hours 52 minutes)

This was an interesting read, especially on the heels of "Superintelligence". Yuval Noah Harari, the author, spends the first half of the book talking about the relationships we have with each other, due to the information we share and our feelings about that information. He points out that information has historically only been created by humans, and our shared belief in that information equates, in some ways, to power. Harari says that AI is able to create its own information (or new ideas/thoughts) without our full intervention or understanding, and have others find value in that information. He suggests that AI has inserted itself as an equal (if not more dominant) partner in our society, and is concerned about its unchecked development. He warns that due to our lack of shared oversight and shared understanding of (and input into) the algorithms behind AI, that we're handing over power and in ultimate danger.

The whole book was interesting. I've read some authors that are really good at distinguishing between facts, results, and interpretations. Unfortunately, Harari doesn't differentiate as well as I would like. I found myself disagreeing with him, based on logical fallacies I saw. I'm not saying he's wrong in some or all of his conclusions. But some aspect of his writing or presentation didn't speak to me, and was distracting.
